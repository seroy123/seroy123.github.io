Learn bounds that adapt to the observed flow and reward useful precision.

Why we explored this. Our early calibration experiments showed that forecast errors carried structure: conditioning residual distributions on motion improved their bounds, and local spatial context helped further. The widest intervals covered more targets, yet did not earn the best SPS score. Those results supported two choices: let the input determine the uncertainty, and train for the score's balance between containing the target and keeping the interval narrow.

Observe the flow→Predict error bounds→Optimize interval quality

Let uncertainty depend on the flow

The first reference used one fixed residual interval per forecast horizon and velocity component. A small neighbor-aware convolutional model could instead use the local flow context to predict asymmetric bounds. We trained lower and upper residual quantiles, then calibrated them with conformalized quantile regression (CQR).

Interval = ŷ + [δlow(x), δhigh(x)]

ŷ: point forecast · x: observed flow context · δ: learned lower and upper point-error offsets.

Train for useful bounds

SPS rewards intervals that contain the target while penalizing excessive width, with a multiplier for forecast accuracy. We kept quantile training and added a smooth approximation of this objective. Smooth interval-membership functions supplied gradients; evaluation still used the hard scorer. With the original inputs held fixed, direct-SPS training improved over CQR alone. Adding input-window summaries and a longer fit improved it further.

Give uncertainty its own learned features

Later experiments trained the forecaster and interval head together, allowing uncertainty training to influence their shared representation. The final SimVP model carries this direction forward with a dedicated late SPS path, followed by learned interval refinement. The early standalone calibration heads were steps toward that design.

Joint-training result under the later scoring protocol

Under v9, a train-only CQR head on a frozen backbone scored validation SPS 42.7046 and local quality 78.4211. A joint model trained from random initialization reached 46.1662 SPS and 78.9916 quality. Calibration selected its epoch and validation remained untouched.

This compares training recipes, including their budgets and objectives. It supports the joint-training direction without isolating a single architectural change.
