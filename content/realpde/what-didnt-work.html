The experiments we moved on from helped shape the final solution.

We explored alternative features, architectures, and augmentations, retaining changes that improved validation performance. We stopped some trials early, usually when their initial validation results did not justify further training. Several ideas improved training metrics or individual scores without improving overall forecast quality. The results below reflect the configurations and training budgets we tested.

Features

More explicit flow geometry. We tested whether giving the uncertainty model a solid mask, distance to the boundary, and vorticity and strain features would improve its error bounds. With the forecaster, folds, seed, and training budget held fixed, validation SPS fell from 61.293 to 61.116. Enlarging the neighborhood reduced it further, to 60.783. Coverage rose, but the intervals widened and their score fell. These particular extra features did not improve the uncertainty model.

Removing the derived features. We tested whether a simpler input would reduce overfitting. Removing temporal differences, local summaries, and global conditioning together lowered quality. A raw-only variant recovered a little by dropping coordinates, but still trailed the full feature stack. Adding an explicit half-cosine time channel to that minimal model also hurt. We retained the derived features.

Architectures

Trying other forecasters. The search extended beyond SimVP: we trained a ConvNeXt U-Net, SwinUNETR, a SimVP model with a parallel Fourier/FNO adapter, and several wavelet, recurrent, and temporal-attention variants. The completed 400-epoch ConvNeXt, Fourier-adapter, and SwinUNETR runs reached local quality scores of 82.0299, 81.7435, and 81.3539. We continued developing the SimVP recipe used in the final submission.

Learning a second correction with WDNO. We tried a wavelet diffusion neural operator as a residual refiner, including joint training and a version on top of a frozen SimVP. The frozen-base experiment produced only tiny changes. In a later fine-tuning run, the best quality equaled the base forecast with the correction gate set to zero: the refinement was effectively disabled. We dropped this extra stage.

CNO3D. We trained a CNO3D model with a dedicated uncertainty path and stopped early after 11 epochs. Its best validation quality was 72.2276, reached at epoch 10.

Comparing WDNO refinement with its own base forecast

We stopped the frozen-SimVP/WDNO run after 390 of 400 planned epochs. Its best refined quality was 81.8078, versus 81.8123 for the base forecast evaluated alongside it. The largest positive difference during training was only +0.0046. We stopped the subsequent fine-tuning run after 65 of 100 planned epochs. Its best quality was 81.8525, equal to the base with a zero correction gate; none of its validation evaluations improved on the corresponding base.

Augmentations

Gaussian input noise. Adding noise at 1% of each recording's velocity standard deviation produced a misleadingly low training loss. The noise inflated the local temporal scale used for normalization, reducing the normalized velocity-MSE term without a matching validation improvement. Best quality was 81.55, about 0.3–0.4 points below the comparable clean run. We removed this augmentation.

Mixing across recordings and resizing larger crops. Broadening mixup to different recordings gave a slightly lower score than using windows from the same recording. Larger crops resized to the model's input grid also underperformed the small crop kept at native resolution. We retained the same-recording mixing and native-crop recipes.

Perturbing temporal Fourier modes. An earlier experiment varied the amplitudes of low-frequency temporal modes to regularize fluctuation energy while preserving mean flow. It slightly improved peak TKE and reduced the training–validation TKE gap, but best overall quality fell from 82.0878 to 82.0392. Improving the targeted diagnostic was not enough to improve the forecast's combined score.
