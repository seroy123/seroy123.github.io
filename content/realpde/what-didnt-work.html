Some useful lessons came from ideas that never reached the final submission.

We tried richer features, alternative forecasters, and more aggressive data augmentation. Several experiments improved training metrics without improving held-out forecasts; others helped one score at the expense of another. This section collects those results, alongside architecture trials whose saved histories are too short to support a conclusion.

Features

More explicit flow geometry. We tested whether giving the uncertainty model a solid mask, distance to the boundary, and vorticity and strain features would improve its error bounds. With the forecaster, folds, seed, and training budget held fixed, validation SPS fell from 61.293 to 61.116. Enlarging the neighborhood reduced it further, to 60.783. Coverage rose, but the intervals widened and their score fell. These particular extra features did not improve the uncertainty model.

Removing the derived features. We also tested the opposite idea: could a simpler input reduce overfitting? Removing temporal differences, local summaries, and global conditioning together lowered quality. A raw-only variant recovered a little by dropping coordinates, but still trailed the full feature stack. Adding an explicit half-cosine time channel to that minimal model also hurt. The useful lesson was to test individual additions and removals; the results did not support stripping out all derived features.

Architectures

Trying other forecasters. The search extended beyond SimVP: we trained a ConvNeXt U-Net, SwinUNETR, a SimVP model with a parallel Fourier/FNO adapter, and several wavelet, recurrent, and temporal-attention variants. The completed 400-epoch ConvNeXt, Fourier-adapter, and SwinUNETR runs reached local quality scores of 82.0299, 81.7435, and 81.3539. These alternatives stayed outside the final recipe. Their different training settings make the scores a record of our search, rather than a controlled ranking of architecture families.

Learning a second correction with WDNO. We tried a wavelet diffusion neural operator as a residual refiner, including joint training and a version on top of a frozen SimVP. The frozen-base experiment produced only tiny changes. In a later fine-tuning run, the best saved quality equaled the base forecast with the correction gate set to zero: the refinement was effectively disabled. The saved experiments did not establish a useful gain from this extra stage.

CNO was tried, but remains inconclusive. A CNO3D model with a dedicated uncertainty path has actual training logs. The recovered history contains only 11 of 1,000 configured epochs, with validation last recorded at epoch 10. That is evidence of an attempt, not enough evidence to reject CNO. Several other alternatives also have incomplete saved histories.

Comparing WDNO refinement with its own base forecast

The frozen-SimVP/WDNO history reaches epoch 390 of 400. Its best refined quality was 81.8078, versus 81.8123 for the base forecast evaluated alongside it. The largest positive difference anywhere in that history was only +0.0046. The subsequent fine-tuning history reaches epoch 65 of 100; its best quality was 81.8525, equal to the base with a zero correction gate. None of its saved evaluations improved on the corresponding base.

Evidence: recovered Colab/Drive histories and run configurations, plus local TAU and PFGNet artifacts. Architecture trial summary. These records extend the older experiment ledger.

Augmentations

Gaussian input noise. Adding noise at 1% of each recording's velocity standard deviation produced a misleadingly low training loss. The noise inflated the local temporal scale used for normalization, reducing the normalized velocity-MSE term without a matching validation improvement. Best quality was 81.55, about 0.3–0.4 points below the comparable clean run. We removed this augmentation. Mixup was disabled in the noise experiment, so this was not a test of adding noise to the final mixup recipe.

Mixing across recordings and resizing larger crops. Broadening mixup to different recordings gave a slightly lower score than using windows from the same recording. Larger crops resized to the model's input grid also underperformed the small crop kept at native resolution. We retained the same-recording mixing and native-crop recipes. The crop comparison changed area and resampling together, so it cannot identify which change caused the difference.

Perturbing temporal Fourier modes. An earlier experiment varied the amplitudes of low-frequency temporal modes to regularize fluctuation energy while preserving mean flow. It slightly improved peak TKE and reduced the training–validation TKE gap, but best overall quality fell from 82.0878 to 82.0392. Improving the targeted diagnostic was not enough to improve the forecast's combined score.
